PREFACE


        In 1992 Professor Tahsin Özgüc asked me to take care of the edition of the cuneiform tablets excavated by him in kārum Kanesh during the year 1991 and the beginning of 1992, when the excavation of the second house investigated in 1991 was completed. I gratefully accepted his invitation and when studying the more than 600 tablets during the following years it turned out that they belonged to three different archives. The first consisted of Kt 91/k 100-284, which according to the excavator was found in 1990 «north of the Elamma house in LII-LIII/126-127 plan square». I hope, my age and health permitting, to publish this archive in the future. The third archive comprised Kt 92/k 188-263, found in 1992 in the house in grid quare LV-LVI/126-127, and it was published by me in 2010 as Kültepe Tabletleri V (TTKY VI/33c), «The Archive of Kuliya, son of Ali-abum». The second and largest archive, comprising Kt 91/k 285-568 and Kt 92/k 94-187, is the archive of Elamma, son of Iddin-Suen, and his family; it is published in this volume. Workon this large archive took many many years, with many visits to the Museum in Ankara and was frequently interrupted due to various administrative and academic duties, including the publication of some other books.[1] Moreover, I gave priority to the publication of the above-mentioned «Archive of Kuliya», which also yielded the first «Kültepe Eponym List» (KEL A), which I edited separately in 2003.
        It is a plesant duty to thank all those who have made this publication possible. In the first place the excavator and his staff, who retrieved these cuneiform documents from a heavily damaged house. Fikri Kulakoǧlu, the current director of the Kültepe excavations, apart from showing me the ruins of Elamma's house in the lower town of Kanesh, provided me with some additional information on the circumstances of the discovery. I am grateful to the directors of the Anadolu Medeniyetleri Müzesi for giving me the opportunity to work in their museum and to the ladies of the Tablet Seksyonu, who made the tablets available to me during my many visits over the years, in particular to Sherife Yilmaz, who also helped me with information on some tablets and carried out some collartions. I am grateful to Cahit Günbatti, Irfan Albayrak and Hakan Erol, of the University of Ankara, who answered questions and helped me with various collations. Ayse Karaduman provided me with information on some seal impressions shared by texts of Elamma and of the Kt n/k archive. 
        The help of the members of «Old Assyrian Text Project», a initiative of Mogens Larsen, was invaluable and exemplary. They gave me access to unpublished archives and both in our meetings and in correspondence we discussed many issues and problems. I owe a special debt of gratitute to Mogens Larsen for making preprints of his edition of the archive of Šalim-Aššur (AKT 6) available and for a critical reading of parts of my manuscript. Bert Kouwenberg contributed valuable remarks on the transliteration and translation of a number of texts and provided me with a copy of the manuscript of his forthcoming Old Assyrian Grammar, to which I refer in my edition simply as Grammar. I used with profit the various Old Assyrian websites created by Thomas Hertel and discussions with him on the interpretation of judicial records have stimulated me, as did Gojko Barjamovic's observations on matters of geography relating to

But I do hope that the result will convince others that cooperation and sharing of data between all those working on «Kültepe tablets» is very profitable for all parties. May the new initiative of the «Kültepe International Meeting» (started in Sept. 2013) stimulate personal contacts and provide a forum for exchange of data, views and scholarly discussion. 
        The publication of new cuneiform texts always requires philological notes and comments on their reading, translation and interpretation, because a simple translation is often not sufficient for their understanding, certainly not for those not initiated in the world of Old Assyrian trade, but he edition of a large archive allows only a limited space for them. The notes added to the texts give, when necessary, short information on the reading and meaning of the words, comment on points of grammar, refer to parallel texts, provide some prosopographic and chronological data, and may refer to the literature. The comments on the texts in the present editions deal in a concise way with the meaning and implication of the texts, but in some cases, where I considered a text especially interesting or important, more elaborate comments were necessary. Realizing the limitations of an archival edition, I have over the years edited a number of important texts from the archive that required a more detailed commentary in articles, often in a Festschrift for a colleague.         I have allowed colleagues to use transcriptions of Elamma's texts, to quote or (in rarer cases) publish them, if they were important for their research, in particular Barjamovic, Dercksen, Hertel and Michel, while Kryszat used their chronological data in Kryszat 2004. References to quotations of the texts are also found in C. Michel's Bibliography under their excavation numbers.
         New texts also acquaint us with new, rare or difficult words and with additional, more specific or clearer attestions of them, not yet or not well treated in our dictionaries, which were completed before most of the texts excavated since 1948 became available. To account for my translations they had to be discussed in notes of lexical content, some of which became long and occupied too much room in this volume. Some could be presented in the above-mentioned publications of a number of important texts, to which the notes in this volume then refer. In other cases I decided to take them out of this volume and to present them in a forthcoming, alphabetically arranged article, «Contributions to Old Assyrian Lexicography», to be published in the journal Orientalia (Roma), to which I refer in the notes to some texts in this volume by means of «Veenhof, Lex., s.v. ».[2]
        The photos acompanying this volume of course can only show a selection of the tablets. Principles of selection were in general the importance of a text or text type, the presence of seal impressions and the state of preservation, to show the basis for the reading of a damaged tablet. The selection also includes some exceptionally well or badly written texts, some large or very small tablets, and it tries to document to some extent the varieties in the style of writing (by professional scribes or amateurs), and the peculiarities of «hands» of the better attested letter writers. The photos also provide material for a good Old Assyrian signlist, which is still missing.
